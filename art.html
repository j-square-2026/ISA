Art

Two directions of looking.

Our art programme is divided into an external branch and an internal branch. One faces the public; the other stays close to the studio. Work travels between them as it matures.

01
External

Exhibitions, commissions and outreach

The external branch is the public face of the Institute. It produces exhibitions, site-specific commissions, publications and talks, often in partnership with museums, universities and civic bodies. Work here is finished, presented and argued about in public.

The branch also runs the Institute's public programme, including open studios, talks and a small press for artist books and exhibition catalogues.

  • Exhibitions
  • Commissions
  • Artist publications
  • Public programme
  • Partnerships
02
Internal

Studio practice, process and critique

The internal branch is where the work is made. Studios, workshops, peer critique and long unstructured time — the parts of an artistic practice that rarely appear in an exhibition but make one possible.

Internal residents are not required to exhibit. Their work is protected by a simple rule: nothing leaves the studio until the artist decides it is ready.

  • Studio residency
  • Peer critique
  • Material research
  • Process documentation
  • Unfinished work

Crossing over

From the studio to the street.

Each year a small number of internal projects are selected, with the artist, to move into the external programme. The move is optional and always collaborative — the Institute does not schedule an artist's debut.

Studio visits

Open critique

Monthly critique sessions with invited artists, scientists and curators from outside the Institute.

Residencies

Long-form time

Residencies of three to twelve months, with no fixed output requirement and full access to the laboratories.

Press

Small editions

A modest publishing arm for artist books, exhibition catalogues and process notes, distributed through the website.
