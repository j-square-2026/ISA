Science

Two ways of asking.

Our science programme is divided into a theoretical branch and an experimental branch. They are not separate departments — they are two temperaments, and they share every question the Institute takes up.

01
Theoretical

Models, mathematics and simulation

The theoretical branch works with the tools of thought: mathematical modelling, computation, information theory and the philosophy of measurement. Its output is often a framework rather than a device — a way of describing how a system behaves before anyone touches it.

Members publish in open preprints, maintain shared software, and run a weekly reading seminar with the experimental branch. An idea is only considered finished here when it can be tested or performed.

  • Mathematical modelling
  • Computational simulation
  • Information theory
  • Philosophy of measurement
  • Algorithmic methods
02
Experimental

Labs, materials and measurement

The experimental branch works at the bench: synthesising materials, building optical benches, prototyping sensors and measuring what the theoretical branch predicts. It is a place of hands, tools, and the productive surprise of a result that disagrees with the model.

Laboratory notebooks are kept open, and every group hosts a rotating public demonstration so that the city can see what is being made here.

  • Materials synthesis
  • Optics & photonics
  • Sensor design
  • Prototyping
  • Measurement & instrumentation

Crossing over

Where the two branches meet.

Every project at the Institute has a named contact in both branches. A theoretical result is taken up by an experimental group; an experimental anomaly is sent back to theory. The shared seminar is where most of our work begins.

Joint seminar

Weekly exchange

Alternating hosts, open to residents and the public. Notes are published as part of the open archive.

Shared archive

Open notebooks

Experimental logs and theoretical working papers are kept together, so that the reasoning behind a result stays visible.

Public lab

Demonstration days

Twice a year the laboratories open to visitors, with demonstrations designed in partnership with the Art programme.
